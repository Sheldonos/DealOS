# Demo Guide Architect

This skill transforms a raw demo framework into a polished, audience-calibrated guide through four phases: review, research, rewrite, and deliver.

## Workflow Overview

1. **Review** the draft — read the document, identify gaps, typos, and structural weaknesses; deliver feedback to the user.

1. **Research** the audience — gather event themes, audience mandates, pain points, and compliance context.

1. **Rewrite** the guide — produce a final version where research is woven into the narrative, not appended.

1. **Deliver** — output Markdown and PDF; summarize what changed and where.

---

## Phase 1: Review the Draft

Read the uploaded document and evaluate it across four dimensions:

| Dimension | What to Check |
| --- | --- |
| **Clarity** | Is the value proposition stated in the audience's language, or in generic product language? |
| **Completeness** | Does each demo step have a "why" — a bridge connecting the action to an audience benefit? |
| **Accuracy** | Are there typos, factual errors, or weak competitive claims? |
| **Structure** | Is the flow logical for a live booth setting where attention is limited? |

Produce a structured feedback document and deliver it to the user. Confirm they want to proceed to the final version before rewriting.

---

## Phase 2: Research the Audience

Before writing the final guide, research the specific event and audience. Read `references/audience-research.md` for the full research framework and search query patterns.

**Key research targets:**

- Event theme and confirmed panel topics — mirror this language in the guide.

- Senior leader public statements about priorities and pain points — use these as talking points.

- Compliance and regulatory requirements the audience faces — connect product capabilities to these.

- Competitive landscape specific to this audience's hard requirements (on-prem, certifications, track record).

**Critical principle:** Research findings must be woven into the existing guide structure — into the opening framing, the talking points at each step, the competitive section, and the closing. Do NOT add a new standalone "Audience Context" section or append findings as a list at the end.

---

## Phase 3: Rewrite the Guide

Use `templates/demo-guide-template.md` as the structural reference. The final guide must:

- Open with the audience's own language and a named problem they are trying to solve.

- Include a one-sentence audience-facing talking point at each demo step.

- Connect competitive weaknesses to the audience's specific hard requirements.

- Close the final demo step on the audience's most pressing concern.

- Be free of typos, passive voice, and generic product language.

**Persona pivot guidance:** If the event draws a mixed audience (e.g., commanders, CIOs, allied partners, acquisition officers), include a brief persona table so the presenter can pivot the pitch based on who is at the booth.

| Persona | Key Value to Emphasize |
| --- | --- |
| [Persona 1] | [Top value] |
| [Persona 2] | [Top value] |

---

## Phase 4: Deliver

Output the final guide as both Markdown and PDF. In the delivery message, summarize what changed and where — do not make the user re-read the entire document to find the changes.

---

## Quality Standards

- Every demo step must have a "so what" for the specific audience.

- Audience language and terminology must appear naturally throughout — not only in a dedicated section.

- The guide must be scannable by a presenter on a busy booth floor: clear headings, bold key terms, visual cue callouts at each step.

- Compliance and regulatory hooks must be present where relevant to the audience.

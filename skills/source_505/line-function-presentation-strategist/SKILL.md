# Line Function Presentation Strategist

This skill provides a framework for building "outside-in" strategic presentations. It forces the narrative to start with how the client makes money (the "Fish Spine") and positions the technology solution as the mechanism to remove friction from that process.

## Core Philosophy: The Fish Spine

1. **The Spine (The Line Function):** How the client actually generates revenue. (e.g., For a rental company: "Equipment leaves the yard on a contract and comes back ready to rent again.")

1. **The Spokes (Support Functions):** Everything else that enables the spine (IT, HR, Finance, QA, Architecture).

1. **The Friction:** Where the spokes slow down the spine. (e.g., "It takes IT 12 months to deliver a feature, which means the field lacks a capability for 12 months.")

**Rule:** If you cannot explain how the client makes money, and how your solution enhances that specific ability, the presentation will fail.

## The 5-Slide Narrative Arc

When generating a presentation using this framework, strictly follow this 5-slide arc:

### Slide 1: The Opener (The Line Function & The Friction)

- **Purpose:** Validate that you understand their business, not just your technology.

- **Left Column (The Line Function):** List 3 specific ways the client generates revenue or measures operational success (e.g., Fleet Utilization, Contract Velocity).

- **Right Column (The Friction):** List the operational or technical bottlenecks slowing down the line function.

- **Anchor:** A strong statement bridging the friction to the solution. (e.g., "The legacy system isn't the problem. The speed at which your team can build on it is.")

- **Constraint:** DO NOT mention your product or solution on this slide.

### Slide 2: The Pivot (Introducing the Solution)

- **Purpose:** Introduce your product/solution as the direct answer to the friction raised in Slide 1.

- **Format:** A strong, simple title and subtitle.

- **Tone:** Positioning the solution as a partner or enabler, not just a tool.

### Slide 3: The Stakeholder Map

- **Purpose:** Map the solution to the specific roles of the people in the room.

- **Format:** Visual diagram (if available) or a structured list.

- **Content:** Explicitly state how the solution aids Developers, DevOps, Architects, and Business Leaders in their specific daily tasks.

### Slide 4: The Capability Proof

- **Purpose:** Translate technical features into productivity outcomes.

- **Format:** A grid or list of 4-6 key capabilities.

- **Content:** Do not just list features. Explain *what the feature allows the team to do faster or safer*. (e.g., Instead of "Auto-generated documentation," write "Auto-generated documentation ensures the architecture is never lost when team members leave.")

### Slide 5: The Closer (The Outcome Chain)

- **Purpose:** Tie the technology directly back to the revenue metrics from Slide 1.

- **Format:** A 3-column outcome chain: "If your team can [Technical Action] -> Then the field can [Operational Outcome] -> And the company earns [Financial Outcome]."

- **Anchor:** A closing statement of alignment. (e.g., "We don't sell software. We help you move equipment.")

## Execution Guidelines for the Agent

1. **Research First:** Before writing any slides, you MUST research the client's business model. Identify their specific "Line Function."

1. **No Product Pitches:** If a slide reads like a feature catalog, rewrite it. Every feature must be attached to a business outcome.

1. **The "Logo Test":** If you removed your company's logo from the presentation, the story should still make perfect sense to the client's executives.

1. **Visual Restraint:** Use clean, modular layouts. Avoid excessive animations or cluttered graphics. Let the strategic alignment carry the weight of the presentation.

# The Institutional Investment Qualification & Portfolio Construction Playbook

## Advanced Hedge Fund and Private Equity: The Complete 25-Step Process

---

## Process Overview

The institutional investment process is a disciplined, multi-phase system designed to:
1. **Qualify opportunities with ruthless rigor**
2. **Construct portfolios that generate absolute returns in any market environment**
3. **Manage risk so no single outcome can be catastrophic**

| Phase | Name | Steps |
|-------|------|-------|
| **I** | Sourcing and Initial Qualification | 1–5 |
| **II** | Deep Due Diligence and Underwriting | 6–12 |
| **III** | Investment Committee Approval and Execution | 13–16 |
| **IV** | Portfolio Construction and Risk Engineering | 17–21 |
| **V** | Value Creation and Ongoing Monitoring | 22–24 |
| **VI** | Exit Strategy and Capital Redeployment | 25 |

When a user asks about any step, produce the relevant documents, templates, and frameworks from
that step. When asked for a full runthrough, walk all 25 steps in order.

## Phase I: Sourcing and Initial Qualification (Steps 1–5)

### Step 1: Investment Mandate Definition and Coverage Universe Construction

Define the precise parameters of the fund mandate before evaluating any opportunity.

**Investment Policy Statement (IPS) — Required Sections:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Investment Objective | Target return, risk budget, benchmark |
| Eligible Asset Classes | Equities, credit, real assets, private equity, derivatives |
| Geographic Scope | Domestic, international, emerging markets |
| Concentration Limits | Max position size, max sector exposure |
| Liquidity Requirements | Minimum liquidity profile of the portfolio |
| Leverage Policy | Gross and net exposure limits |
| ESG Policy | Exclusion lists, ESG scoring requirements |
| Governance | IC composition, quorum, voting rules |

Hedge fund analysts are assigned to verticals of 25–30 companies within 2–3 sub-industries.
Coverage lists are built in Excel with market cap, EV, and forward earnings estimates for every
company in the vertical, organized into comp tables.

---

### Step 2: Idea Generation and Proactive Sourcing

**Hedge Fund Sources:** Quantitative screens (e.g., stocks below historical P/E with improving
earnings revisions), macro top-down analysis, sell-side research for consensus views worth
challenging, industry conferences, expert networks.

**PE Sources:** Direct outreach to management teams, investment banker relationships, industry
consultants, portfolio company management identifying adjacent M&A targets.

**Idea Generation Log / Sourcing Tracker:**

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Date Sourced | Date first identified |
| Source Channel | Banker, screen, proprietary, expert network |
| Company / Asset | Name and brief description |
| Sector / Vertical | Industry classification |
| Initial Thesis | One-sentence investment rationale |
| Estimated Valuation | Entry price or valuation range |
| Status | Screening / Active Diligence / Passed / Closed |
| Pass Reason | If passed, why |

---

### Step 3: Initial Screening and Mandate Triage

Apply the **5-Minute Triage Checklist** to every incoming opportunity:

1. Does the opportunity fall within the defined investment mandate (sector, geography, size)?
2. Is the current valuation within a range that could support the target return?
3. Is there a specific, articulable thesis — a reason *this* opportunity is mispriced *now*?
4. Is there a clear catalyst or path to value realization within the target holding period?
5. Are there any immediate disqualifying factors (regulatory, litigation, governance red flags)?

Any unambiguous "no" = log it, pass it, protect analyst time.

---

### Step 4: Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) Execution

For private transactions. Key provisions to negotiate:

- Definition of "Confidential Information"
- Exclusions (publicly available; independently developed information)
- Permitted disclosures (to advisors and financing sources)
- Term (typically 12–24 months)
- Standstill provisions (restricting unsolicited approaches to employees or customers)

---

### Step 5: Deal Alert / Preliminary Investment Committee Review

**Deal Alert Memo (2–3 Pages):**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Opportunity Overview | Company, sector, transaction type, source |
| Preliminary Thesis | Why this is an attractive investment |
| Preliminary Valuation | Estimated entry multiple and implied return |
| Key Risks | Top 3–5 risks identified at this stage |
| Proposed Next Steps | Diligence workstreams and budget request |
| Recommendation | Proceed to deep diligence / Pass |

Purpose: not to approve the investment, but to authorize analyst time and budget for deeper
diligence and to submit a non-binding indication of interest.

## Phase II: Deep Due Diligence and Underwriting (Steps 6–12)

### Step 6: Non-Binding Letter of Intent (LOI) / First Round Bid

**LOI Structure:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Proposed Valuation | Enterprise value range (e.g., 8.0x–9.0x EBITDA) |
| Deal Structure | Equity check, debt financing, management rollover |
| Key Assumptions | Revenue growth, margin trajectory, capex |
| Diligence Requirements | Outstanding items needed before binding offer |
| Exclusivity Request | Request for exclusivity period (if proprietary) |
| Timing | Expected timeline to binding offer |

---

### Step 7: Virtual Data Room (VDR) Access and Diligence Workstreams

**Comprehensive Due Diligence Request List (DDQ) — by Workstream:**

| Workstream | Key Items Requested |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Financial** | Audited financials (5 years), management accounts, budget vs. actuals, working capital analysis, capex schedule, debt schedule |
| **Commercial** | Customer contracts, customer concentration, churn data, pipeline, pricing history, competitive win/loss |
| **Legal** | Corporate structure, material contracts, IP ownership, litigation history, regulatory licenses, employment agreements |
| **Tax** | Tax returns (5 years), deferred tax positions, transfer pricing, NOL carryforwards |
| **Operational** | Org chart, key employee retention, IT systems, cybersecurity, supply chain contracts |
| **Environmental** | Environmental permits, known liabilities, Phase I/II reports |

---

### Step 8: Financial Modeling and Scenario Analysis

**The Five Components of an Institutional Financial Model:**

1. **Historical Analysis (3–5 years):** Normalized income statement, balance sheet, cash flow. Adjust for non-recurring items to arrive at true EBITDA.
2. **Operating Model (5-year forecast):** Revenue built from operational drivers (volume × price, customer count × ARPU). Costs as fixed vs. variable. Margin bridge from current to target.
3. **Valuation Module:** Trading comps, precedent M&A comps, DCF, LBO analysis. For hedge funds: multiple-based target price with catalyst timeline.
4. **Scenario Analysis:** Base, upside, downside — each reflecting a coherent set of macro and company-specific assumptions.
5. **Sensitivity Tables:** Two-variable tables showing returns across entry/exit multiples, revenue growth, and margin assumptions.

**Scenario Analysis Summary Template:**

| Metric | Downside | Base | Upside |
|--------|----------|------|--------|
| Revenue CAGR | 3% | 8% | 14% |
| EBITDA Margin (Exit Year) | 18% | 22% | 26% |
| Exit Multiple | 7.0x | 9.0x | 11.0x |
| Gross IRR (PE) | 12% | 22% | 35% |
| MOIC (PE) | 1.6x | 2.8x | 4.5x |
| Implied Upside (HF) | -15% | +35% | +75% |

---

### Step 9: Commercial Due Diligence (CDD)

Conducted by third-party specialists (Bain, LEK, AlixPartners) via primary research with customers,
competitors, and industry experts.

**CDD Report Structure:**

| Section | Key Questions |
|---------|---------------|
| Market Size and Growth | True TAM? Realistic serviceable market? Structural growth drivers? |
| Competitive Dynamics | Key competitors? Sustainable moat? Widening or eroding? |
| Customer Analysis | Customer concentration? NPS? Key churn drivers? |
| Product/Service Quality | How does the product compare to alternatives? Switching cost? |
| Management Assessment | Can management execute the plan? Are they aligned? |

---

### Step 10: Operational Due Diligence (ODD)

Studies show the majority of hedge fund failures are attributable to operational failures, not
investment losses. ODD is a genuine risk mitigation tool, not a box-checking exercise.

**ODD Checklist:**

| Category | Key Assessment Areas |
|----------|---------------------|
| Governance and Structure | Legal entity, board composition, conflicts of interest, fiduciary duties |
| Risk Management | Risk limits framework, VaR methodology, drawdown triggers, leverage monitoring |
| Technology and Cybersecurity | Trading system reliability, data security, disaster recovery, vendor risk |
| Valuation and Pricing | Independence of valuation function, third-party pricing agents, illiquid asset policies |
| Compliance and Regulatory | Registrations, compliance manual, personal trading policies, AML/KYC |
| Service Providers | Prime broker quality, fund administrator independence, auditor reputation |
| Key Person Risk | Succession planning, retention agreements, team depth |

---

### Step 11: Preliminary Investment Memorandum (PIM)

Typically 30–50 pages. Synthesizes all diligence into a single coherent narrative.

**PIM Structure:**

1. **Executive Summary** (2–3 pages) — Transaction overview, thesis, key financials, recommendation. Must stand alone.
2. **Transaction Overview** — Purchase price, financing structure, management rollover, special terms.
3. **Company Overview** — History, products/services, customer base, geography, org structure, management bios.
4. **Market and Industry Analysis** — Market size, growth, key trends, competitive dynamics, Porter's Five Forces.
5. **Investment Thesis and Value Creation Plan** — Specific articulation of mispricing (HF) or operational improvements (PE).
6. **Financial Analysis** — Historical performance (normalized), management vs. deal team projections, valuation analysis.
7. **Risk Factors and Mitigants:**

| Risk | Probability | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|----------|------------|
| Customer concentration (top 3 = 40% revenue) | Medium | High | Contractual lock-ups; diversification plan post-close |
| Key person dependency (founder-led) | High | High | Retention package; succession plan pre-close |
| Macro recession | Medium | Medium | Defensive revenue model; variable cost structure |
| Competitive disruption | Low | High | Strong IP moat; ongoing R&D investment |

8. **Exit Strategy** — Initial analysis of exit options, expected timing, target return multiples.
9. **Proposed Project Plan** — Next steps, third-party consultants, timeline to binding offer, budget request.

---

### Step 12: Final Confirmatory Due Diligence

All outstanding questions resolved. Key documents required before proceeding:

- Quality of Earnings (QoE) Report — independent accounting firm validates normalized EBITDA
- Legal Due Diligence Report
- Environmental Phase I/II Report (if applicable)
- Debt Commitment Letters from Lenders
- Final Management Presentations

---

## Phase III: Investment Committee Approval and Execution (Steps 13–16)

### Step 13: Final Investment Memorandum (FIM)

Updates the PIM with all confirmatory diligence findings. Key additions over the PIM:

- QoE findings and impact on normalized EBITDA
- Final debt financing terms and capital structure
- Resolved legal and regulatory issues
- Final management retention and incentive plan
- Updated financial model reflecting all diligence findings
- Definitive exit analysis with identified buyer universe

---

### Step 14: Final Investment Committee Approval

**IC Meeting Best Practices:**

- Deal team presents for 30–45 minutes, followed by structured Q&A
- IC members receive FIM at least 24–48 hours in advance
- Decisions documented in IC minutes, including dissenting views

**IC Explicit Vote Questions:**

1. Is the investment thesis sound and differentiated?
2. Are the financial projections reasonable and supported by diligence?
3. Is the risk/reward profile acceptable given the portfolio's existing exposures?
4. Is the proposed deal structure appropriate?
5. Is the management team capable of executing the value creation plan?

---

### Step 15: Binding Offer, Negotiation, and Definitive Agreements

**Key Negotiation Points:**

- Representations and warranties (scope and survival period)
- Indemnification provisions and caps
- Closing conditions and regulatory approvals
- Working capital peg and adjustment mechanism
- Earn-out provisions (if applicable)
- Management incentive plan structure

Key agreements: Share Purchase Agreement (SPA), Shareholders' Agreement, Management Incentive Plan.

---

### Step 16: Closing, Funding, and Onboarding

Capital drawn from LPs (PE) or prime brokerage account (HF). Portfolio company formally onboarded
into the firm's portfolio management system. Dedicated deal team assigned to manage through the
holding period.

## Phase IV: Portfolio Construction and Risk Engineering (Steps 17–21)

### Step 17: Conviction-Based Position Sizing

**Volatility-Adjusted Conviction Sizing Matrix:**

| Tier | Label | Capital Allocation | Criteria |
|------|-------|-------------------|----------|
| 1 | High Conviction Core | 8–15% of NAV | Highest-quality thesis, strongest edge, most liquid |
| 2 | Core | 4–8% of NAV | Strong thesis, good liquidity, moderate risk |
| 3 | Satellite | 1–4% of NAV | Emerging thesis, higher risk, or limited liquidity |
| 4 | Exploratory | <1% of NAV | Early-stage, monitoring position |

Within each tier, actual size is adjusted by expected volatility to keep risk contribution consistent.

**Fractional Kelly Criterion:** `f* = (bp - q) / b` where b = net odds, p = P(win), q = P(loss).
Institutional practice: size at 1/4 to 1/2 Kelly to account for model uncertainty.

**Hard Position Limits (Non-Negotiable):**

- No single position exceeds 15% of NAV (unless explicitly approved by the IC)
- No single sector exceeds 30% of gross exposure
- No single country exceeds 40% of gross exposure (global funds)

---

### Step 18: Correlation and Factor Exposure Analysis

**Monthly Portfolio Correlation Matrix and Factor Loadings Report:**

1. Pairwise correlation matrix of all positions (trailing 12M and 36M)
2. Factor loadings per position and aggregate portfolio (equity beta, duration, credit spread sensitivity, commodity sensitivity, FX sensitivity)
3. Concentration alerts when any factor exposure exceeds pre-defined limits

**Shared Exposure Limits:**

- Total equity beta: capped at 0.3 net for market-neutral strategies
- Total credit spread DV01: capped at defined dollar amount per basis point
- Positions with correlation > 0.7 are treated as a single position for concentration limits

### Step 19: Macro Regime Allocation — The All-Weather Framework

**The Four-Quadrant Macro Matrix:**

| Quadrant | Economic Condition | Outperforming Asset Classes |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Q1: Rising Growth, Rising Inflation** | Expansion with price pressure | Commodities, TIPS, Equities |
| **Q2: Rising Growth, Falling Inflation** | Goldilocks expansion | Equities, Corporate Credit, Real Estate |
| **Q3: Falling Growth, Rising Inflation** | Stagflation | Gold, Commodities, TIPS, Short Equities |
| **Q4: Falling Growth, Falling Inflation** | Recession / Deflation | Nominal Government Bonds, Cash, Long Duration |

**Risk Parity Principle:** Allocate equal *risk* (not equal capital) to each quadrant. Bonds require
larger capital allocation to achieve equal risk contribution with equities.

**Indicative All-Weather Asset Allocation:**

| Asset Class | Capital Weight | Risk Contribution |
|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| Global Equities | 30% | ~25% |
| Long-Term Government Bonds | 40% | ~25% |
| Inflation-Linked Bonds (TIPS) | 15% | ~25% |
| Gold and Commodities | 15% | ~25% |

### Step 20: Stress Testing, Scenario Analysis, and VaR

**Historical Stress Scenarios (Mandatory):**

| Scenario | Period | Key Market Moves |
|----------|--------|-----------------|
| Global Financial Crisis | Sep 2008 – Mar 2009 | S&P 500 -55%, Credit spreads +600bps, VIX >80 |
| COVID-19 Shock | Feb 2020 – Mar 2020 | S&P 500 -34% in 33 days, Oil -60% |
| 2022 Rate Shock | Jan 2022 – Oct 2022 | 10Y UST +300bps, S&P 500 -25%, Bonds -20% |
| Dot-Com Bust | Mar 2000 – Oct 2002 | NASDAQ -78%, Value outperformed Growth 50%+ |
| 1994 Bond Massacre | Feb 1994 – Nov 1994 | 10Y UST +250bps, EM -30% |

**Hypothetical Stress Scenarios:**

- Sudden 200bps rise in interest rates (duration-sensitive positions)
- 30% decline in equity markets (net long exposure)
- Credit spread widening of 300bps (credit positions)
- USD appreciation of 15% (international positions)
- Oil price decline of 50% (energy-related positions)

**VaR Framework:** Report at 95% and 99% confidence levels, 1-day and 10-day horizons. VaR
describes *minimum* losses in *normal* markets. Stress testing is the primary tool for tail risk.

### Step 21: Pre-Defined Exit Rules and Scale-Up Triggers

**Position Mandate / Trade Ticket (Required Before Deploying Capital):**

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Investment Thesis | The specific, falsifiable thesis for the position |
| Target Price / IRR | The expected value realization |
| Catalyst Timeline | When the thesis is expected to play out |
| Thesis Invalidation Criteria | Specific events or data that would prove the thesis wrong |
| Maximum Drawdown Trigger | Loss level (e.g., -15% from cost) that triggers mandatory review |
| Hard Stop-Loss | Loss level (e.g., -25% from cost) that triggers automatic exit |
| Scale-Up Trigger | Gain level (e.g., +10% with thesis intact) that triggers adding to position |
| Maximum Position Size | The largest this position can grow to |
| Liquidity Assessment | Days to liquidate at 20% of ADV |

**The Re-Underwrite Rule:** When a position hits its drawdown trigger, answer: "If we did not own
this position today, would we buy it at the current price with the current information?"
- "No" → mandatory exit
- "Yes" → formal re-underwriting of the thesis, documented in writing, before maintaining position

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Portfolio Summary | NAV, gross/net exposure, top 10 positions, attribution |
| Performance Attribution | Alpha vs. beta, sector attribution, factor attribution |
| Thesis Tracking | Each position: On Track / Monitoring / At Risk |

**Rebalancing Triggers:**

- Position weight drifts >2.5% above target allocation → trim
- Position volatility exceeds target band by >20% → reduce
- New position increases a factor exposure beyond its limit → hedge or reduce elsewhere
- Position thesis assessed as "At Risk" → mandatory IC review

### Step 24: Performance Attribution Analysis

**For Hedge Funds (Brinson-Hood-Beebower Model):**

- **Allocation Effect:** Did sector/factor allocation decisions add value?
- **Selection Effect:** Did individual security selection within each sector add value?
- **Interaction Effect:** Combined effect of allocation and selection.

**For Private Equity (IRR Attribution):**

- Revenue Growth — contribution to IRR from top-line growth
- Margin Expansion — contribution from EBITDA margin improvement
- Multiple Expansion — contribution from entry-to-exit multiple change
- Leverage — contribution from debt paydown and financial engineering
- Dividends/Distributions — contribution from cash distributions during holding period

**Benchmark Comparison:**

- Long/short equity HF → HFRI Equity Hedge Index
- PE fund → Public Market Equivalent (PME) vs. S&P 500 or Russell 2000

## Phase VI: Exit Strategy and Capital Redeployment (Step 25)

### Step 25: Exit Planning, Execution, and Capital Redeployment

Exit planning begins at the time of investment, not at the end of the holding period. The best
exits are planned, prepared for, and executed from a position of strength.

**PE Exit Routes:**

| Exit Route | Description | Optimal Conditions |
|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **IPO** | List portfolio company on public exchange | Strong equity markets; company has scale and growth story; public comps at premium |
| **Strategic Sale** | Sell to corporate acquirer | Strategic buyer willing to pay synergy premium; unique capabilities or market position |
| **Secondary Buyout** | Sell to another PE firm | Further growth potential; financial buyer sees additional value creation runway |
| **Dividend Recapitalization** | Refinance debt to extract cash dividend | Strong cash flows; favorable debt markets; used to return capital without full exit |
| **Management Buyout** | Sell to existing management team | Management has strong conviction and access to financing |

**Hedge Fund Exit Criteria — exit when any of these conditions are met:**

1. Thesis has played out and position has reached target price
2. Thesis has been invalidated by new information
3. Position has hit its hard stop-loss
4. A better use of capital has been identified (opportunity cost exit)
5. Position's risk contribution to the portfolio has grown beyond its mandate

**Capital Redeployment:** Upon exit, capital is immediately returned to the sourcing funnel
(Step 2). The deal team conducts a formal **post-mortem** documenting what went right, what went
wrong, and lessons to incorporate into the investment process. Store in institutional memory and
review during future IC meetings for similar opportunities.

## The All-Weather Profitability Framework: Six Core Principles

**Principle 1: Risk Parity Over Capital Parity.**
Allocate equal *risk* to each macro regime, not equal capital. Prevents the portfolio from being
dominated by the risk of any single asset class.

**Principle 2: Diversification Across Uncorrelated Return Streams.**
The portfolio must contain multiple sources of alpha that are genuinely uncorrelated — not just
different names in the same factor bucket.

**Principle 3: Pre-Defined Rules Remove Emotion.**
Every position has pre-defined entry, scale-up, and exit rules. The portfolio manager's job is to
execute the process, not make ad-hoc decisions under stress.

**Principle 4: Stress Testing is the Primary Risk Tool.**
VaR describes normal markets. Stress testing describes the markets that actually destroy portfolios.
The portfolio must be sized so even the worst historical scenario produces a survivable drawdown.

**Principle 5: Continuous Monitoring and Rebalancing.**
The portfolio is a living system. Thesis drift, factor exposure creep, and correlation changes must
be monitored continuously and corrected systematically.

**Principle 6: The Exit is Part of the Entry.**
Every investment is made with a clear exit thesis. The path to realization is mapped before capital
is committed.

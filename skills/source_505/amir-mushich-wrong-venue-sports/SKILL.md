# Amir Mushich "Wrong Venue" Sports Photography

This skill provides a framework for writing professional AI image generation prompts based on the "Wrong Venue, Right Athlete" methodology. This style places elite athletes performing at their absolute peak skill level in locations where that sport would never normally occur, creating a powerful visual juxtaposition.

## Core Philosophy

The visual DNA of this style relies on four pillars:

1. **The Juxtaposition (Wrong Venue)**: The environment must be a recognizable, mundane, or culturally opposite civilian space (e.g., grocery store, church, Victorian parlor, bowling alley).

1. **The Athlete (Right Athlete)**: The subject must be wearing full, authentic, professional athletic gear for their specific sport and performing a "peak action" movement (mid-throw, mid-jump, full sprint).

1. **Documentary Authenticity**: The lighting and camera work must feel like raw, on-location documentary photography, not a polished studio setup.

1. **Environmental Framing**: The environment is a character. Foreground elements and wide-to-normal lenses are used to ground the surreal action in reality.

## The "Wrong Venue" Prompt Framework

Prompts in this style should follow this structured anatomy:

### 1. Variable Declaration

Declare the core variables at the top:`[SPORT/ATHLETE] = [e.g., Olympic Fencer]``[VENUE] = [e.g., Grocery Store Produce Aisle]`

### 2. Role Assignment

Assign the AI a specific photographic role:`Act as a gritty, on-location sports documentary photographer shooting an editorial campaign.`

### 3. Goal Statement

State the core juxtaposition clearly:`Goal: Generate a surreal, high-contrast documentary photograph placing a [SPORT/ATHLETE] performing peak action inside a [VENUE].`

### 4. Phase-Based Instructions

Break the prompt down into distinct phases:

- **PHASE 1: THE JUXTAPOSITION & ACTION** - Define the specific athletic move and how it interacts with the incongruous environment.

- **PHASE 2: AUTHENTICITY & WARDROBE** - Detail the professional gear, sweat, and intensity of the athlete.

- **PHASE 3: COMPOSITION & FRAMING** - Specify the camera angle, foreground elements, and how the environment frames the subject.

- **PHASE 4: LIGHTING & ATMOSPHERE** - Describe the available environmental light, flash, or motion blur.

- **PHASE 5: TECH SPECS** - Provide the camera, lens, and film stock details.

## Key Techniques to Apply

- **Motion Blur & Speed**: For speed sports (cycling, running), instruct the AI to use heavy motion blur to imply speed breaking the static environment.

- **Available Light**: Emphasize the use of the venue's natural lighting (e.g., harsh fluorescents in a store, warm ambient light in a church, or direct paparazzi flash).

- **Foreground Depth**: Always include elements from the venue in the extreme foreground (e.g., produce bins, stair railings) to create depth.

- **Vertical Orientation**: This style is native to social media; specify a vertical aspect ratio (e.g., 9:16 or 4:5).

## Workflow

1. **Understand the Request**: Identify the sport and the desired "wrong venue." If the user only provides one, autonomously suggest the other to maximize contrast (e.g., if they ask for a rugby player, suggest a delicate antique shop).

1. **Select a Template**: Choose the appropriate template from the `references/templates.md` file based on the desired mood (e.g., Action/Blur, Static/Tension, Silhouette).

1. **Customize the Prompt**: Fill in the variables and adapt the phases to fit the specific sport and venue.

1. **Deliver the Prompt**: Present the finalized prompt to the user, ready for their AI generator.
